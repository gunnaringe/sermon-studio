From the recording of a service to sermon files

You use Sermon Studio in the browser, with nothing to install: the work is done on the server. Upload the recording of the whole service, mark where the sermon starts and ends, and press Save. You get the sermon as FLAC, MP3 and Opus, at an even, standard loudness, ready for a podcast or the church's website.

Free and open source. No adverts, no tracking.

How it works

Five steps, each in the studio as the pictures show it.

  1. 1. Upload the recording

    Drop the recording of the service on the recordings page, or choose it. Most audio and video files work: WAV, FLAC, MP3, M4A and more. The date is taken from the file name. A recorder can also drop its files in a folder, or a sound technician can send them with a code, without a login.

  2. 2. Mark the sermon

    The whole service is shown as a waveform. Drag across the sermon, or move its start and end. Cut out a cough or a song in the middle. Listen to the joins before you save. The original recording is never changed.

  3. 3. Choose how it sounds

    Processing sets mono or stereo, noise reduction, leveling and the loudness. The church's defaults are used until you change them. An intro and an outro, such as a jingle and a closing word, can go around the sermon, at the same loudness.

  4. 4. Save

    Save keeps the edit and makes the files you chose. You can follow the work, or close the browser: it goes on without you. A transcript, if the church wants one, is made afterwards.

  5. 5. Download and share

    The finished files are on the recordings page, with the intro and outro or the sermon only. Share for download gives a code and a link, so someone without a login can download the sermon.

What it does

  • Even loudness. The sermon is noise-reduced, leveled and brought to podcast loudness (−19 LUFS), measured with EBU R128.
  • The original is kept. Files are only deleted after their copy has been checked to be identical.
  • Several churches. Each church has its own recordings, members, tags, logo, intro and outro.
  • On a phone too. In English, Norwegian bokmål and nynorsk, light or dark.
  • Transcripts. The sermon as text and captions, made on the server after the files, with speech models made for Norwegian and Swedish too. Read it before you publish it.
  • Shared with a code. A code or a link lets someone without a login send a recording in, or download a finished sermon, for as long as you decide.

Straight from the mixing desk

The sound technician needs no login. Make an upload share in the studio and give them its link or code. They open the upload page on the computer at the mixing desk, or on a phone, and send the recording of the service straight to the church: large files are fine, and an upload that is interrupted continues where it stopped.

You decide how long the code works, how many files it takes and how large they may be, and you see each upload in the studio. The recording arrives among the church's recordings, ready to be edited.

For churches

Do you record your services and want the sermons online? Ask for an invite, with the name of your church: we set up the church in the studio, and you then invite the others who will use it. Have you been invited? Open the link you got: you can make an account there if you have none.

It costs nothing. Sermon Studio is not run for profit: write to us, and your church gets a free account.

Run it yourself

Sermon Studio is free and open source, under the Apache 2.0 licence. Would you rather not use the hosted studio? Run it on one computer, or on your own server.

The desktop app

The desktop app runs the studio on one computer, without a server or a login. It is for Windows, macOS and Linux, and has everything it needs built in.

Download from GitHub

The app is not signed, so Windows and macOS warn the first time you start it. On Windows, choose More info, then Run anyway. On macOS, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway. After that it starts like any other program. Each release lists the checksum of every file.

On your own server

The same studio as here, with Docker, for one church or several, with your own login (OpenID Connect, such as Pocket ID).

About Sermon Studio

I made Sermon Studio for my own church. Every Sunday the service is recorded, and someone has to cut out the sermon, even out the sound and publish it, often late in the evening in a busy week. I wanted a way to do that which is easy and quick: open the recording, mark the sermon, press Save.

It is free and open source. If it can help your church too, you are welcome to use it.
